SpaceX Headlines Create the RKLB Opportunity
Bullish · On Watch RKLB · NASDAQ · PT $62 November 22, 2026
Published by LIRIA RESEARCH · Independent Equity Research

Rocket Lab has fallen approximately 60% from its highs, with consensus treating SpaceX's post-IPO dominance narrative as an extinction-level event for the small-launch sector. The logic appears straightforward: if SpaceX can launch anything, anywhere, at any price point, why would anyone choose Electron? We believe this framing fundamentally misreads how launch markets actually function.

The selloff has compressed RKLB to roughly 4x forward revenue—a valuation that prices in permanent competitive marginalization. Yet the operational reality tells a different story: Rocket Lab's launch cadence continues accelerating, its Space Systems backlog has grown substantially, and the recent Mars burn milestone demonstrates technical capabilities that extend far beyond small-sat delivery. The market is conflating headline risk with fundamental risk.

When Citi declares SpaceX's lead "increasingly a 20-year advantage," they're describing Starlink's moat—not the dynamics of commercial launch procurement. These are related but distinct markets, and the distinction matters enormously for RKLB's investable thesis.

"SpaceX's backlog saturation is RKLB's demand catalyst. When Falcon manifests stretch 18+ months and Starship prioritizes internal payloads, dedicated small-sat providers become the only viable path to orbit for time-sensitive customers."
Market Dynamics
The Backlog Saturation Thesis

SpaceX's commercial success has created a counterintuitive problem: they have more demand than they can service. Falcon 9's manifest is reportedly stretched 18-24 months for non-priority customers, while Starship development continues consuming engineering bandwidth and launch infrastructure. Meanwhile, Starlink deployment—SpaceX's most profitable vertical—commands priority access to available slots.

This creates addressable market share for providers who can offer dedicated launches with predictable timelines. Rideshare economics favor SpaceX on pure cost, but mission-specific requirements increasingly favor dedicated small-launch providers. Customers needing precise orbital insertion, rapid deployment, or classified payload handling cannot wait two years for a Falcon slot.

Electron's value proposition isn't competing with Falcon on price—it's competing on availability, flexibility, and mission assurance. These are different markets serving different customer needs.

RKLB remains the only publicly-traded company besides SpaceX with a proven, operational orbital launch system and consistent flight heritage. This distinction matters more than the market appreciates.

The Mars burn milestone achieved earlier this year demonstrates propulsion capabilities that position Photon spacecraft for deep-space missions—a market segment where SpaceX's focus on LEO mega-constellations creates natural white space. Interplanetary mission support represents a genuine differentiation vector that's largely absent from current valuation models.

Business Mix
Space Systems: The Underappreciated Revenue Engine

Consensus fixation on launch economics obscures RKLB's transformation into a vertically-integrated space company. The Space Systems segment—encompassing spacecraft components, reaction wheels, solar panels, and satellite buses—now represents a substantial and growing portion of total revenue.

This diversification reduces single-segment risk and creates multiple paths to profitability. Space Systems carries structurally higher margins than launch services and benefits from long-term contracts with government and commercial customers. The segment's growth also validates RKLB's acquisition strategy, integrating capabilities from prior deals into a cohesive supply chain.

Catalyst Path
Neutron and the Medium-Launch Opportunity

Neutron development represents RKLB's bid for the medium-launch segment—a market currently dominated by Falcon 9 and potentially disrupted by Starship. The vehicle targets roughly 13,000 kg to LEO with partial reusability, addressing the payload gap between Electron's small-sat focus and Falcon's heavy-lift capability.

Execution risk here is substantial. Neutron's development timeline has extended, and first-launch success is not guaranteed. However, the addressable market for a proven Falcon alternative is significant, particularly among customers seeking launch provider diversification for strategic or regulatory reasons. National security customers, in particular, have expressed explicit interest in maintaining multiple qualified providers.

We estimate Neutron's first orbital attempt in late 2027. Success would meaningfully expand RKLB's serviceable market; failure would pressure the stock but wouldn't invalidate the core Electron/Space Systems thesis.

Valuation
What the Market Is Pricing

At $45.42, RKLB trades at approximately 4x estimated 2027 revenue—a compression that implies either permanent margin suppression or secular demand decline. Neither assumption aligns with observable operational trends.

Our $62 price target assumes modest multiple expansion to roughly 5.5x forward revenue as Neutron development de-risks and Space Systems growth compounds. This remains conservative relative to historical trading ranges and peer valuations in adjacent aerospace segments.

The current price effectively assigns zero option value to Neutron and minimal strategic premium to RKLB's position as the sole SpaceX alternative with proven orbital capability. We view this as overly punitive given the company's execution track record.

Risk Assessment
What Could Derail the Thesis
  • Neutron Development Failure

    Extended delays or first-launch failure would pressure the stock significantly and validate bear concerns about RKLB's ability to compete at scale. The medium-launch segment is essential to the long-term growth narrative.

  • SpaceX Rideshare Pricing Aggression

    If SpaceX substantially reduces rideshare pricing or expands dedicated small-sat capacity, Electron's value proposition narrows. Starship's eventual economics could pressure the entire small-launch category.

  • Government Budget Compression

    Defense and civil space budgets face fiscal pressure. Reduced NASA or DoD procurement would impact both launch and Space Systems segments, particularly higher-margin government contracts.

  • Dilution from Capital Raises

    Neutron development requires continued investment. Additional equity raises could dilute existing shareholders, though the company's current cash position provides runway through initial Neutron milestones.

Liria Verdict
Bullish · On Watch — PT $62
The market is treating SpaceX dominance as a zero-sum death sentence for RKLB. We see it differently. SpaceX's backlog saturation, Starlink prioritization, and strategic customer demand for provider diversification create structural demand for capable alternatives. Rocket Lab is the only public company positioned to capture that demand. The 60% drawdown prices in existential risk that misreads competitive dynamics—Electron and Space Systems continue executing while Neutron development progresses toward a potentially transformative catalyst. We maintain our bullish stance with heightened monitoring of Neutron milestones and SpaceX capacity expansion signals.
