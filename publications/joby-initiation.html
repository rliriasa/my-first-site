Joby Aviation: The FAA Certification Bet That Could Reshape Urban Transit
High Risk · Futurist Bet JOBY · NYSE · PT $11.5 November 09, 2026
Published by LIRIA RESEARCH · Independent Equity Research

Joby Aviation sits at the precise inflection point where regulatory approval transforms from theoretical optionality into binary catalyst. The company has completed over 90% of the FAA's certification requirements for its S4 aircraft, positioning it as the clear frontrunner in the race to commercialize electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) technology in the United States. At $6.18 per share, the market prices roughly a 35-40% probability of successful certification and commercial launch—a discount we believe fundamentally misprices both the regulatory progress achieved and the strategic moat being constructed.

Our thesis rests on three interconnected observations: first, that Joby's certification pathway is more advanced than any competitor and follows a uniquely de-risked trajectory through its Department of Defense contracts; second, that the Toyota manufacturing partnership represents approximately $900 million in committed capital that dramatically alters unit economics at scale; and third, that first-mover advantage in urban air mobility creates network effects that will prove difficult to replicate. We initiate coverage with a price target of $11.5, representing approximately 86% upside, while acknowledging the binary nature of this investment.

"The market treats all eVTOL companies as equivalent lottery tickets. They are not. Joby has accumulated roughly 1,400 hours of flight testing, secured Air Carrier certification under Part 135, and built manufacturing infrastructure—milestones that took competitors years to even approach."
Regulatory Pathway
Decoding the FAA Certification Maze

The FAA certification process for novel aircraft represents one of the most demanding regulatory hurdles in any industry. Joby is pursuing Type Certificate (TC) approval under 14 CFR Part 23, the same pathway used for traditional fixed-wing aircraft, which requires demonstrating compliance with approximately 300 individual airworthiness standards. As of Q3 2026, Joby has completed substantiation for an estimated 270+ of these requirements, placing it well ahead of competitors who remain in earlier certification phases.

What differentiates Joby's approach is the parallel pursuit of Part 135 Air Carrier certification—the operational license required to transport passengers commercially. The company received this certification in 2024, making it the first eVTOL developer to hold both manufacturing and operational approvals. This dual-track strategy means Joby can begin revenue-generating operations immediately upon Type Certificate issuance, rather than facing a secondary multi-year approval process.

The Department of Defense relationship provides an underappreciated validation pathway. Through the Agility Prime program, Joby aircraft have been operating in military contexts since 2023, accumulating real-world flight data under conditions that parallel commercial use cases. This DoD operational experience feeds directly into FAA certification evidence, creating a feedback loop that competitors pursuing purely civilian pathways cannot replicate.

Manufacturing Economics
The Toyota Partnership Changes Everything

When Toyota invested $400 million in Joby in 2020, followed by additional tranches totaling approximately $900 million, the transaction was reported primarily as financial backing. The strategic reality is far more significant: Toyota has embedded its production engineering capabilities directly into Joby's manufacturing DNA. Toyota's Production System—the methodology that revolutionized automotive manufacturing—is being adapted for aerospace applications at Joby's Marina, California facility.

Traditional aerospace manufacturing operates on fundamentally different economics than automotive production. A Boeing 737 requires roughly 50,000 labor hours to assemble; a Toyota Camry requires approximately 30. Joby's S4 aircraft, with its relatively simpler architecture (six tilting electric motors, carbon fiber composite airframe, no complex fuel systems), sits between these extremes. Toyota's involvement targets a production cadence closer to automotive norms—a proposition that would be dismissed as fantasy without Toyota's demonstrated expertise.

Current estimates suggest Joby is targeting initial production costs of approximately $1.3-1.5 million per aircraft, declining to roughly $800,000-1 million at scale volumes of several hundred units annually. If achievable, these economics would support ticket prices competitive with premium ground transportation in congested urban corridors—the fundamental requirement for mass-market adoption.

Market Positioning
First-Mover Dynamics in Urban Air Mobility

The urban air mobility market remains largely theoretical, with total addressable market estimates ranging from $30 billion to $1 trillion depending on assumptions about regulatory timelines, infrastructure buildout, and consumer adoption curves. We view such projections skeptically. What matters for Joby specifically is the credible near-term addressable market: premium airport transfers in congested metropolitan areas.

Joby has secured vertiport agreements in New York, Los Angeles, and Dubai—the three highest-value initial markets. The New York route connecting Manhattan to JFK airport, currently a 60-90 minute ground journey costing $100+ by car service, could be completed in approximately 7 minutes via eVTOL at a projected price point of $150-200. This represents genuine time-value arbitrage for business travelers, a demographic with demonstrated willingness to pay premiums for convenience.

Network effects in air mobility operate differently than in ground transportation. Vertiport locations are constrained by zoning, noise regulations, and airspace management—creating natural barriers to entry once established. A competitor entering three years after Joby's commercial launch would face not only certification delays but also a locked-up infrastructure landscape in premium markets. This dynamic parallels early airline route economics, where first-mover landing rights created durable competitive advantages.

The Delta Air Lines partnership, announced in 2022 with a $60 million investment and commitment to launch JFK-Manhattan service, provides both distribution channel access and operational credibility. Delta's involvement signals that established aviation players view eVTOL as complementary rather than threatening—a strategic positioning that could accelerate regulatory acceptance and consumer trust.

Competitive Landscape
Mapping the eVTOL Field

The eVTOL sector includes over 200 companies globally, but meaningful competition narrows to approximately five players with credible paths to near-term certification: Joby, Archer Aviation, Lilium, Vertical Aerospace, and EHang. Each pursues distinct regulatory strategies and aircraft architectures, making direct comparison complex.

Archer Aviation represents the most direct competitive threat, pursuing similar FAA certification with backing from United Airlines and Stellantis. The Stellantis manufacturing partnership, while valuable, lacks the aerospace-adjacent experience that Toyota brings from its decades in advanced materials and precision manufacturing.

EHang's Chinese certification, achieved in 2024, demonstrates that regulatory approval is achievable but offers limited read-through to FAA processes, which operate under fundamentally different evidentiary standards and political dynamics.

This positions the company to reach anticipated commercial launch without requiring dilutive capital raises, assuming certification timeline holds and no major unforeseen expenditures emerge.

Risk Assessment
What Could Go Wrong
  • FAA Certification Delay or Denial

    The most material risk. Novel aircraft certification has historically experienced significant delays—the Boeing 787 Dreamliner took roughly three years longer than initially projected. Any technical finding requiring design modification could push Joby's timeline by 12-24 months, straining cash runway and competitive positioning. Complete certification denial, while unlikely given progress to date, would be catastrophic.

  • Battery Technology Limitations

    Current lithium-ion battery energy density constrains the S4's range to approximately 100 miles with reserves—adequate for urban routes but limiting expansion options. Battery degradation rates in high-cycle commercial operations remain partially unknown, potentially requiring more frequent and costly replacements than modeled. A safety incident involving battery failure could trigger industry-wide regulatory reexamination.

  • Infrastructure Buildout Delays

    Commercial operations require vertiport networks that don't yet exist at scale. Permitting, construction, and grid connection for charging infrastructure face local regulatory hurdles that Joby cannot directly control. Partner commitments (Delta, real estate developers) could weaken if certification timelines slip, creating chicken-and-egg dynamics.

  • Consumer Adoption Uncertainty

    No mass-market precedent exists for urban air taxis. Consumer willingness to pay projected price points, comfort with autonomous or semi-autonomous flight, and tolerance for noise and weather-related cancellations remain unvalidated assumptions. Early operational challenges could damage brand perception before network effects establish.

  • Competitive Acceleration

    Archer or international competitors achieving certification ahead of expectations could erode first-mover advantages. However, the certification process is sufficiently transparent that surprise acceleration appears unlikely—Joby's lead is structural rather than informational.

Liria Verdict
High Risk · Futurist Bet · PT $11.5
Joby Aviation represents the highest-quality exposure to the urban air mobility thesis available in public markets. The combination of regulatory progress, strategic partnerships, and manufacturing infrastructure creates a moat that competitors will struggle to replicate within relevant time horizons. We view the current $6.18 price as embedding excessive skepticism about certification probability—our analysis suggests approximately 65-70% likelihood of successful 2027 Type Certificate issuance versus the roughly 35-40% implied by current valuation. This is not a stock for risk-averse portfolios; it is a calculated asymmetric bet on a transportation paradigm shift. Position sizing should reflect the binary nature of the catalyst, but for investors with appropriate risk tolerance, Joby offers compelling risk-reward at current levels. We initiate with a price target of $11.5, representing approximately 86% upside to our probability-weighted fair value.
