Starlink's 150Mbps Assault Changes the Calculus — But Carrier Alignment Is the Real Moat
High Risk · Futurist Bet ASTS · NASDAQ · PT $38 November 24, 2026
Published by LIRIA RESEARCH · Independent Equity Research

ASTS shares dropped approximately 18% last week after SpaceX announced a 40x upgrade to Starlink's direct-to-cell service, targeting roughly 150Mbps to unmodified smartphones. The market read this as an extinction-level event for AST SpaceMobile. We disagree.

The more important signal came two days later: Verizon CEO Hans Vestberg's pointed pushback that there's "no secret sauce" in Musk's carrier ambitions, and that incumbents have no intention of becoming SpaceX's wholesale customers. This isn't corporate posturing — it's a preview of how the direct-to-cell market will actually bifurcate.

AST's competitive moat was never speed. It's carrier alignment. We maintain our $38 price target while acknowledging the risk profile has evolved significantly.

"The direct-to-cell battle isn't AST vs. Starlink — it's carriers vs. SpaceX, with AST as the weapon of choice for incumbents who refuse to cede their customer relationships to Elon Musk."
Context
The 150Mbps Headline Obscures the Real Competition

SpaceX's announced upgrade is technically impressive. Moving from approximately 4Mbps to 150Mbps would put Starlink's direct-to-cell offering on par with terrestrial LTE in many markets. For AST SpaceMobile, whose Block 2 satellites currently deliver estimated speeds of 10-20Mbps to standard smartphones, this appears devastating on paper.

But raw throughput has never been the primary constraint in satellite-to-phone economics. The binding variables are: spectrum access, regulatory approval, carrier revenue share, and critically, who owns the customer relationship.

SpaceX's path to 150Mbps requires either acquiring substantial licensed spectrum (expensive, scarce) or convincing regulators to allow aggressive spectrum sharing (politically fraught, especially post-Ligado). Their current service operates in narrow bands borrowed from T-Mobile under strict power limits. Scaling to 150Mbps at scale requires a fundamentally different regulatory posture.

Strategic Analysis
Why Verizon's Pushback Matters More Than Starlink's Specs

Vestberg's comments weren't offhand. They followed weeks of industry speculation that SpaceX intends to eventually bypass carrier partnerships entirely and become a facilities-based carrier itself — using satellite infrastructure to offer direct consumer plans.

For Verizon, AT&T, and international incumbents, this represents an existential threat that AST SpaceMobile simply doesn't pose. AST's business model is explicitly carrier-aligned: they provide satellite infrastructure, carriers retain the customer relationship, and revenue shares are negotiated as partnerships rather than wholesale arrangements.

The strategic question isn't whether Starlink can deliver faster speeds — it probably can. The question is whether carriers will let SpaceX anywhere near their customer base when an alternative exists that doesn't threaten their core business model.

Competitive Dynamics
Two Markets, Not One

We expect the direct-to-cell market to bifurcate into two distinct segments with limited overlap:

In markets where carriers have strong customer relationships and regulatory influence — essentially all developed markets outside the US — AST's partnership model is structurally advantaged. Carriers will actively resist Starlink's expansion while facilitating AST's.

Operational Update
Constellation Progress Remains On Track

AST's Block 2 constellation buildout continues largely as planned. The company has approximately 25-30 satellites operational as of Q3 2026, with commercial service live in limited geographies through AT&T and Vodafone partnerships.

Cash runway remains the primary near-term constraint. With an estimated $180-220M in quarterly cash burn and roughly $400-500M in available liquidity, the company likely requires additional capital within 12-18 months. We expect this to come via a combination of strategic investment from carrier partners and potentially favorable debt facilities secured against contracted revenue.

The commercial ramp has been slower than management's most optimistic projections but roughly in line with our base case. Early user feedback suggests the technology works as advertised, with connection reliability being the primary area for improvement.

Valuation
Price Target Maintained at $38

Our $38 price target reflects a risk-adjusted DCF assuming AST captures approximately 3-5% of the addressable direct-to-cell market by 2030, with ARPU in the range of $3-5 per subscriber per month flowing to AST via carrier revenue shares.

The Starlink upgrade announcement increases execution risk but doesn't fundamentally alter the addressable market size or AST's structural advantages with carrier partners. If anything, SpaceX's aggressive posture may accelerate carrier adoption of AST as a defensive measure.

We apply a roughly 40% probability-weighted discount to our base case valuation, reflecting technology execution risk, capital requirements, and competitive uncertainty. This yields a risk-adjusted fair value of approximately $38, representing roughly 48% upside from current levels.

Risk Assessment
Updated Risk Matrix
  • Capital Requirements & Dilution

    AST will almost certainly need to raise additional capital before reaching cash flow breakeven. Terms and timing are uncertain, and significant dilution remains possible if operational milestones slip or market conditions deteriorate.

  • Technology Execution at Scale

    Scaling from dozens to hundreds of satellites while maintaining service quality is non-trivial. Any major satellite failures or persistent connectivity issues could damage carrier relationships irreparably.

  • Starlink Competitive Pressure

    If SpaceX successfully navigates regulatory hurdles and achieves 150Mbps at scale, some carrier partners may reconsider their AST commitments despite strategic concerns. Speed differentials matter at the margin.

  • Regulatory & Spectrum Risk

    Direct-to-cell services operate in a evolving regulatory environment. Adverse rulings on spectrum sharing or interference standards could affect both AST and Starlink, though carrier relationships provide AST some buffer.

  • Carrier Partner Concentration

    While AST has 45+ carrier partners, meaningful revenue concentration exists among top-5 partners. Loss of a major partner would be significant but not catastrophic given pipeline depth.

Liria Verdict
High Risk · Futurist Bet — PT $38
The market is treating Starlink's 150Mbps announcement as a death knell for AST SpaceMobile. We see it as confirmation that the direct-to-cell market is real and large enough for SpaceX to pursue aggressively — which paradoxically strengthens AST's value proposition to carriers who now have even more reason to fear SpaceX's ambitions. The competitive moat isn't speed, it's alignment. Carriers will pay a premium for infrastructure that doesn't threaten to disintermediate them. AST provides that; Starlink increasingly doesn't. Maintain price target at $38, acknowledging elevated near-term volatility as the market digests competitive dynamics.
